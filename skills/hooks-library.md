# Skill: Hooks Library

Hook **patterns**, not hook copy. Each entry is a shape to fill with this
business's own fear, desire, mechanism and proof — read from the connected
website, the Knowledge Vault and the market research, never from this file.

This file used to hold a swipe list written for one specific company: a
residential builder, its region, its contract terminology, its numbers. Every
deployment of the platform was handed that list as "proven hooks", so the copy
agent reached for that industry's fears and jargon regardless of what business
it was writing for. A hook that names the wrong industry is not a weak hook, it
is a wrong one — and the fastest way to produce it is to ship somebody else's
swipe file as a skill.

---

## THE SHAPES

### Cost of inaction
`"<what it costs>. Here's how to never let that happen."`
Name the specific, quantified loss the audience already fears. Works when the
market is problem-aware and the loss is measurable.

### Hidden mechanism
`"<Category> hides the <problem> in <specific place>."`
Name where the damage actually happens — a line item, a step, a habit — in
terms an insider would recognise. Earns authority because only someone who
knows the work could write it.

### Track record
`"<Number>. <Number>. <Number>. That's not luck — that's a system."`
Three concrete figures this business can substantiate. Fails immediately if any
number is rounded up or borrowed.

### Risk reversal
`"<The thing that scares them> shouldn't be your risk to carry."`
Take the named fear off the reader and put it on the business. Only usable when
the offer genuinely does that.

### Contrarian
`"Most <audience> don't have a <assumed problem>. They have a <real problem>."`
Reframe what the market believes about itself. The strongest cold-traffic shape,
and the easiest to get wrong: the reframe has to be true and provable.

### Identity
`"The <role> who <admirable thing> shouldn't still be <undignified thing>."`
Speak to who they are, not what they buy. Handle carefully — Meta prohibits
asserting personal attributes about the viewer, so speak to the situation.

### Process transparency
`"You'll have <specific access>, not <generic substitute>."`
Trade an abstraction for something concrete the reader can picture.

### Aspiration (use sparingly, always grounded)
`"<The thing they picture> — let's <concrete first step>."`
Never the opener for cold traffic. Anchor it to an action, not a feeling.

---

## HOOK CONSTRUCTION CHECKLIST

1. Does it name a specific fear or desire in the first six words?
2. Is there a concrete number, or a detail only this business could supply?
3. Could a competitor run it unchanged? If yes — rewrite it.
4. Under ~12 words and readable at a glance?
5. Does it survive alone above the "See more" fold?

Gate 3 is the one that matters. A hook that passes every other gate and fails
this one is a category claim, and the category does not buy anything.
